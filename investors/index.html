The problem

Centralized fact-checking doesn't scale — and isn't trusted

A handful of institutions can't keep pace with the volume of claims made every day, and a single arbiter's verdict is only as credible as people's trust in the arbiter.

◐

One arbiter, zero transparency

Traditional fact-checks are a black box — you see the verdict, never the methodology behind it.

⏱

Too slow for the feed

By the time a claim is reviewed, it has already reached millions — and the correction reaches a fraction of them.

⚖

No auditable methodology

There's rarely a reproducible way to ask "why this verdict?" — let alone verify the answer yourself.

The product

One scoring engine, everywhere trust is needed

The same deterministic verdict — surfaced wherever people, journalists, and researchers already are.

Why now

The conditions have changed

Three shifts make a transparent, crowd-audited layer more necessary — and more buildable — than it was even a few years ago.

01
Trust in centralized institutions is declining

People increasingly want to verify claims themselves, not be told what to believe by a single authority.

02
Platforms face growing pressure to label content

Regulatory and public pressure is pushing platforms toward transparent, auditable moderation signals.

03
AI-generated content is accelerating the problem

As synthetic content grows, provenance and evidence-backed verdicts become infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.

04
Edge compute makes it economically viable

Running a deterministic scoring engine at the edge (Cloudflare Workers) keeps this cheap to operate at internet scale.
